# Paper 6: Dual-Channel Circulation — Coupling Cost-Offset and Labor Exchange

**Core thesis**: the public-asset pool has two exits — one to the production side (offsetting production costs) and one to the labor side (exchanging leisure labor). The two channels coexist without conflict, jointly letting the system return from the diminishing-return region to the high-marginal-return region without collapsing
**Tools**: dual-channel flow, coupled dynamics, negative feedback
**Falsifiable corollary**: if either channel clogs (costs cannot be offset, or labor cannot be exchanged), the public-asset pool silts up and the system falls back to the old path of "surplus clearing"

---

## Abstract

The previous papers discussed "commodity-boundary control," "labor as credential," and "leisure labor" separately. This paper strings them into a complete circulation structure: **the public-asset pool has two exits.** The first leads to the **production side** — the surplus offsets the next round's production costs, lowering the production side's cost structure; the second leads to the **labor side** — public assets exchange leisure labor, gaining maintenance and appreciation, then flow back to the pool. This paper argues: these two channels are not either-or, but **two coexisting, non-conflicting exits** — together they form a closed loop that maintains steady state through the natural circulation of public assets. The elegance of this loop: it needs no market mechanism to drive it and no planning mechanism to distribute it, relying only on the public-asset buffer layer to complete all circulation.

## 1. The Public-Asset Pool: A Middle Buffer Layer

Imagine the "surplus moved out of the commodity category" as a **buffer pool.** This pool has inputs and outputs.

- **Input**: surplus products at zero marginal return (moved out of the commodity category);
- **Output**: two channels.

The key question: **where do the outputs go?** If there is only one exit (say, destruction), the pool silts up; if there are two exits, the pool can flow.

## 2. Channel One: Offsetting Production Costs (the production side)

The first channel flows public assets **back to the production side**:

- Surplus raw materials, energy, and idle facilities are called from the public-asset pool **free or at low cost**;
- The next round of production no longer needs to buy these resources from the market;
- The production side's cost structure is optimized.

**Effect**: no fiscal subsidy, no price intervention — only "recycling" the surplus into the public-asset pool and letting the next round of producers call it at low cost — **costs fall, marginal return recovers, and the system returns to the high-marginal-return region.**

## 3. Channel Two: Exchanging Leisure Labor (the labor side)

The second channel flows public assets **out to the labor side**:

- The pool's resources are open to those who contribute leisure labor;
- After labor input, new public resources are produced (environmental improvement, facility maintenance, community services);
- The new public resources re-enter the pool.

**Effect**: public assets are not static inventory, but continuously appreciate through labor input — garbage becomes greenery, wasteland becomes parks, idle facilities become service nodes.

## 4. The Coupling of Two Channels: A Complete Closed Loop

The two channels combine into a complete closed loop:

```
zero marginal return → move out of commodity category → enter public-asset pool
                                                          ├─→ ① offset production costs → costs fall → marginal return recovers
                                                          └─→ ② exchange leisure labor → maintain & appreciate → back to pool
```

**The coupling logic of the two channels**:

- Channel one (production side) is responsible for **"recovering marginal return"** — sending the system back to the high-marginal-return region;
- Channel two (labor side) is responsible for **"maintaining public assets"** — keeping the pool from silting up, continuously appreciating.

**One manages "inflow," one manages "outflow" — the two channels jointly maintain the system's steady state.**

## 5. Why It Is "Natural Optimization," Not "Planning"

The most elegant part of dual-channel circulation is that it **needs no central planning**:

- Where resources flow is decided not by some committee, but by the **marginal-return signal**;
- Resources automatically flow out of zero-marginal-return fields (no profit);
- Resources automatically flow into positive-marginal-return fields (pursuing profit).

This is equivalent to a **thermodynamic system's spontaneous diffusion driven by temperature difference** — resources flow from "low-potential regions" (surplus) to "high-potential regions" (scarcity), needing no external command, only correct boundary conditions.

**You are not designing "where resources should go," but "a boundary condition that lets resources know where to go on their own."**

## 6. Falsifiable Corollary

> **Proposition**: if both channels — "offsetting production costs" and "exchanging leisure labor" — are open, the public-asset pool can flow, and the system can naturally return from the diminishing-return region to the high-marginal-return region without collapsing.
> **Contrapositive**: if either channel clogs (the production side refuses to call public assets, or no one joins labor exchange), the pool silts up, the surplus cannot be absorbed, and the system falls back to the old path of "destroy / subsidize / crisis-clearing."

The converse is falsifiable: if a system with both channels open still falls into surplus crisis, then "commodity boundary" is not the only source of surplus, and there is a deeper structural problem.

## Conclusion

Dual-channel circulation is this framework's **circulatory system.** One channel recovers marginal return (production side), one channel maintains public assets (labor side), and the two channels couple through the "buffer layer" of the public-asset pool, letting the system naturally return to steady state without collapsing. **It needs neither market nor planning — only a correct boundary condition.**
