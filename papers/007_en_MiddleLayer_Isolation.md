# Paper 7: Middle-Layer Isolation — Coupling and Decoupling Two Mechanisms

**Core thesis**: the "direct mixing" of capitalism and communism causes the two to erode each other; the correct move is not to make them shake hands, but to build a "middle layer" that decouples them — each running in its own valid region, exchanging signals and materials through the middle layer without direct collision
**Tools**: system decoupling, interface isolation, buffer layers, state switching
**Falsifiable corollary**: if the middle layer is absent (the two mechanisms directly interface), market signals are distorted and public resources consumed; if the middle layer exists, each runs independently without interference

---

## Abstract

Historically, capitalism and communism were treated as "opposite ends," and every "fusion" attempt fell into one dilemma: **direct mixing causes the two to erode each other.** Public assets directly join market competition, distorting price signals; fiscal subsidies directly backstop loss-making enterprises, consuming public resources; cheap land and tax breaks directly support capital accumulation, and public resources are plundered by capital. This paper proposes: the root problem is not "whether to fuse," but "the way of fusing is wrong" — **the two were forcibly stuffed into one channel, directly interfacing, with no buffer.** The correct move is to build a **middle layer** that decouples the two: the market mechanism runs freely in its valid region, the public mechanism takes over in the failed region, and the two exchange "marginal-return signals" and "public assets" through the middle layer without direct collision.

## 1. The Disease of "Direct Mixing"

Under the current system, capitalism and communism **interface directly**, with no buffer:

- Public assets **directly join market competition** (state enterprises compete with private enterprises for the same cake);
- Social welfare **directly subsidizes market costs** (fiscal backstopping of loss-making enterprises);
- Public resources **directly support capital accumulation** (cheap land, tax breaks).

This "direct mixing" produces three diseases:

1. **Market signals are distorted** — public assets are not priced by market logic, so price signals fail;
2. **Public resources are consumed** — fiscal subsidies are unsustainable, public assets drain away;
3. **The two interfere** — market efficiency falls, public assets drain, and neither side wins.

## 2. The Middle Layer's Design

The middle layer's role is **to keep the two from touching directly, and instead let each exchange through the middle layer**:

| Left (market mechanism) | Middle isolation layer | Right (public mechanism) |
|---|---|---|
| Market competition | ← marginal-return signal → | trigger moving out of commodity category |
| Price signal | ← public-asset pool → | labor-exchange usage rights |
| Capital accumulation | ← cost offset → | public-asset return |
| Profit pursuit | ← leisure labor → | public-resource appreciation |

**The middle layer is a "one-way glass"**:

- From the market side, public assets do not join market competition (no price distortion);
- From the public side, market signals do not directly decide public-resource distribution (not held hostage by capital);
- The only things exchanged through the middle layer are three: **marginal-return signal, public assets, and labor time.**

## 3. Why "Isolation" Beats "Fusion"

History has two "fusion" attempts, both failed:

- **Planned-economy fusion**: absorb the whole market into planning — but it destroyed the efficiency signal;
- **Welfare-state fusion**: stuff public welfare into the market — but it distorted the price signal.

Their common error: **trying to "fuse" two mechanisms into one.** But the two mechanisms' operating logics are fundamentally different:

- The market mechanism wants **profit maximization**;
- The public mechanism wants **fairness maximization**;
- The two competing directly in one channel necessarily erode each other.

**This paper's move is not "fusion," but "isolation"** — not trying to make two mechanisms into one, but letting them **each keep its own operating logic, switching through the middle layer only at the boundary.**

## 4. Five Concrete Problems the Middle Layer Solves

1. **State-private conflict**: state enterprises no longer directly join market competition, but act as public-asset managers; private enterprises compete only in high-marginal-return regions, no longer squeezed by cheap state resources.
2. **Fiscal unsustainability**: welfare spending is no longer a bottomless pit, but supported by the labor-exchange protocol in the public-asset pool.
3. **Surplus-capacity crisis**: the surplus is no longer cleared through price wars, but automatically moved into the middle layer and treated as public assets.
4. **Unemployment**: workers eliminated by the market exchange public-asset usage rights through public labor in the middle layer.
5. **Distribution**: distribution is no longer decided by market alone or central planning alone, but completed automatically by the "labor-asset exchange protocol."

## 5. Falsifiable Corollary

> **Proposition**: if an effective middle layer exists (isolating the market and public mechanisms, exchanging only signals and assets), the two can each run effectively within one system without eroding each other, and the system stays stable.
> **Contrapositive**: if the middle layer is absent or co-opted by one side (the middle layer becomes a market appendage or a power tool), the two mechanisms collide directly again, and the system returns to the "mutual erosion" mixed state.

The converse is falsifiable: if a system with a middle layer still shows "distorted market signals + drained public resources," then the middle layer's isolation function has failed, and the middle layer's own design must be examined.

## Conclusion

Capitalism and communism are not enemies — they just **cannot shake hands directly**, and need a middle layer to shake hands for them. And that middle layer is the protocol of "commodity-boundary control + public-asset/labor exchange." **Once the handshake succeeds, the two can each do their own thing without interference, and without wasting each other.**
