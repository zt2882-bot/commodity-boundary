# Paper 12: From *Das Kapital* to "Go Out and Do a Small Job" — The System Entry's User Interface

**Core thesis**: a whole social-system state-transfer protocol derived from *Das Kapital* only truly lands when it reduces to an "experienceable, comparable, choosable daily decision." And that entry is one sentence: "Instead of scrolling short videos, go out and do a small job."
**Tools**: user interface, behavioral decision, system entry
**Falsifiable corollary**: if an ordinary person cannot understand and join the system through one sentence, the system's "user interface" fails, and the framework stays at the elite-deduction layer, unable to spread

---

## Abstract

This series started from the eight-node chain of *Das Kapital* and went all the way to commodity-boundary control, the public-asset/labor exchange protocol, middle-layer isolation, and the execution layer. But all these deductions stay at the "system designer" level. This paper proposes a plain standard: **a system that an ordinary person cannot understand and join through one sentence is not a landable system, but an elite's intellectual game.** And this series happens to have such an entry, explainable in one sentence:

> **"Instead of scrolling short videos, go out and do a small job."**

This paper argues: this sentence is the whole framework's "user interface" — it needs no prior knowledge, no understanding of *Das Kapital*, cybernetics, or marginal return, only a comparison of two behaviors, to let an ordinary person join the system.

## 1. A System Needs an Entry

Any system, however complex internally, needs an **entry** — an interface where a user "gets started on first contact."

- Operating systems have desktops;
- Networks have browsers;
- Markets have wallets.

So what is the entry of a "social-system state-transfer protocol"? If the entry is "first finish *Das Kapital*, then understand cybernetics, then grasp marginal return" — then this system can never spread, because it puts the threshold at the endpoint.

**A landable system's entry must be low enough to "explain in one sentence."**

## 2. Why This Sentence Works as the Entry

"Instead of scrolling short videos, go out and do a small job" — this sentence works as the entry because it does three things at once:

1. **Compare**: it puts two behaviors side by side — "scrolling short videos" (passive consumption) vs. "doing a small job" (active exchange);
2. **Judge**: it embeds a judgment — the former consumes (attention, time, health), the latter produces (public-asset usage rights, physical function);
3. **Call**: it calls an action — "go out," not "keep scrolling."

**Any ordinary person, seeing this sentence, without understanding *Das Kapital*, can directly know: "oh, I can use my time this way."**

## 3. Behind This Sentence Is the Whole Framework, Compressed

This sentence looks plain, but behind it is the entire framework's logic, compressed:

- **"go out and do a small job"** = leisure labor (Paper 5's endogenous fuel);
- **"a small job"** = public-asset maintenance (Paper 4's labor-as-credential);
- **"doing a small job"** = the labor-to-usage-right exchange (Paper 6's dual channel);
- **"instead of scrolling short videos"** = shifting from "passive consumption" to "active exchange" (Paper 9's re-pairing the failed).

**One sentence compresses this series' entire causal chain from *Das Kapital* to public assets, to leisure labor, to physical function, to system steady state.**

## 4. The System's "User Stickiness": The Body's Own Positive Feedback

A system needs "stickiness" to retain users. And this system's stickiness relies not on algorithms, addiction, or coercion — but on the **body's own natural "exercise-health" positive feedback**:

- Going out to do a small job → physical activity → function maintained → medical burden reduced;
- People with better function can do more work → gain more usage rights → more willing to keep participating.

**This positive feedback is not designed; it is the body's own.** It is the fundamental difference between this system and every "algorithm-retains-users" platform — it does not need to extract your attention; it even gives you back your health.

## 5. Falsifiable Corollary

> **Proposition**: if an ordinary person can understand and join the system through the sentence "instead of scrolling short videos, go out and do a small job," then the system has a landable user interface, and the framework moves from "elite deduction" into "mass accessibility."
> **Contrapositive**: if this sentence cannot make ordinary people understand or act (people still choose scrolling over going out to work), then the system's user interface fails, and the framework stays at the elite-deduction layer.

The converse is falsifiable: if the "labor-for-usage-right" incentive is insufficient to make ordinary people give up "passive consumption," then the system's "incentive structure" needs adjustment (e.g., the usage right is not attractive enough, or labor measurement is too cumbersome).

## Conclusion

From *Das Kapital*'s 2.3 million characters to the six words "go out and do a small job" — this series' complete path is: **compressing a grand social-system theory into a daily choice everyone can make.**

And that is precisely the watershed between a "landable system" and an "elite's intellectual game." **If a system cannot make an ordinary person understand it in one sentence and be willing to act, then however grand it is, it remains a blueprint no one reads.**
