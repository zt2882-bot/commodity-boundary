# Paper 4: The Public-Asset/Public-Labor Exchange Protocol — Labor as Credential

**Core thesis**: the surplus portion moved out of the commodity category should neither be destroyed (waste) nor distributed free (tragedy of the commons), but treated as a public asset exchanged with public labor as the credential — not sold, not free, only exchanged by labor
**Tools**: exchange protocols, the tragedy of the commons, non-monetary settlement, credential design
**Falsifiable corollary**: if the "labor-as-credential" protocol cannot prevent free-riding (free) or monopoly (selling), it fails and the surplus falls back into the dilemma of "destroy or commons tragedy"

---

## Abstract

Paper 2 moved the surplus "out of the commodity category," but did not answer "how to handle it afterward." This paper answers: **public assets are exchanged by public labor.** This is a "three-no" protocol — not sold (selling = inequality, the rich own everything), not free (free = tragedy of the commons, everyone abuses and no one maintains), only exchanged by labor (whoever contributes labor gains usage rights). This paper argues: this "three-no" protocol simultaneously avoids the two traps of "market" and "planning," landing on a third solution — **labor as credential.** Its essence is replacing "money" with "labor" as the distribution medium, keeping the efficiency of "distribution by contribution" while avoiding the injustice of "distribution by capital."

## 1. Three Solutions to the Problem

Once moved out of the commodity category, the surplus faces the question "how to distribute it." Historically and logically, there are only three solutions:

| Solution | Logic | Fatal flaw |
|---|---|---|
| **Sell** (marketize) | price and sell | the rich own everything, inequality |
| **Free** (communalize) | supply without discrimination | tragedy of the commons, everyone abuses, no one maintains |
| **Labor exchange** (this paper) | whoever contributes labor gains usage rights | needs a reliable labor-measurement system |

The first two are the two extremes that have failed repeatedly in history. This paper's third is **a path between them**: not sold (avoiding inequality), not free (avoiding the commons tragedy), but using "labor" as the sole exchange credential.

## 2. Formalization: Labor as Credential

Let the public-asset pool be $A$, a participant's labor amount be $L$, and their gained usage right be $U$. The exchange protocol's core rule:

$$U_i \propto L_i \quad \text{(usage right proportional to labor amount)}$$

**Three constraints**:

1. **Non-monetary**: $U_i$ is not priced in money — public assets have no price;
2. **Non-zero-price**: $U_i$ is not free — one must contribute $L_i$ to gain it;
3. **Direct exchange**: $L_i \to U_i$ passes through no intermediary (no government distribution, no market pricing).

The essence of this protocol is: **replace "money" with "labor" as the distribution medium of public assets.**

## 3. Why "Labor as Credential" Avoids Both Traps

**Avoiding the "selling" trap (inequality)**:

- The selling logic is "whoever has money owns," and wealth distribution is unequal;
- The labor logic is "whoever works owns," and everyone has labor capacity (especially leisure time).

**Avoiding the "free" trap (commons tragedy)**:

- The free logic is "use without paying," so everyone abuses and no one maintains;
- Labor's price is "must contribute to use," so the user is also the maintainer.

**The brilliance of "labor as credential" is that it binds "using public assets" and "maintaining public assets" into the same thing.** You clean the beach's garbage (labor), and you gain the beach's usage right (credential) — maintenance itself is the path to usage.

## 4. A Real Prototype: Volunteer Points

This protocol already has prototypes in reality, just not unified under one theory:

- Community volunteer points: do community service for public-service priority;
- Scenic-area volunteers: clean/maintain in exchange for free visits;
- River-chief/forest-chief systems: bind public-resource maintenance to specific participants;
- Garbage-sorting points: sort waste for community public-resource usage rights.

The commonality of these practices is the direct "labor → usage right" exchange. This paper's contribution is unifying these scattered practices under the single protocol "labor as credential."

## 5. A Question That Must Be Answered: How to Measure Labor

The Achilles' heel of "labor as credential" is **measuring labor**:

- Who records labor? (the recorder)
- How is labor quantified? (the metric)
- Can records be forged? (anti-forgery)

Without solving this, "labor as credential" degrades into "whoever shouts loudest gets the most credential." And the key to solving it is a **tamper-proof, traceable labor-recording system** — exactly what Paper 8 (the execution layer) must unfold, and where "event anchoring" technology connects.

## 6. Falsifiable Corollary

> **Proposition**: if a reliable labor-measurement system exists, the "labor-as-credential" protocol can simultaneously avoid "inequality" (selling) and "commons tragedy" (free), achieving sustainable public-asset distribution.
> **Contrapositive**: if labor can be forged or cannot be measured, the protocol degrades into some variant of "selling" or "free," falling back into one of the two traps.

The converse is falsifiable: if a community with a labor-measurement system still shows "commons tragedy" or "monopoly," then "labor as credential" is insufficient and needs supplementary mechanisms.

## Conclusion

"Labor as credential" is the third solution for handling "the surplus moved out of the commodity category." It neither sells nor gives free, but exchanges usage rights with labor — **binding "using public assets" and "maintaining public assets" into the same act.** Its only Achilles' heel: whether labor can be measured trustworthily. And that is exactly what the next layer must solve.
