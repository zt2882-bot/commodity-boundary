# Paper 9: Re-Pairing Failed Assets and Failed Labor — Reconstructing the Distribution Mechanism

**Core thesis**: the "outdated products" eliminated by the market and the "uncompetitive workers" squeezed out of the market are both elements the current system judges as "failed." The true meaning of commodity-boundary control is to let these two kinds of "failed elements" re-pair in a non-market public system, forming a parallel distribution mechanism
**Tools**: resource re-pairing, parallel distribution mechanisms, failure judgment
**Falsifiable corollary**: if "failed assets" and "failed labor" cannot re-pair in the public system, they can only be destroyed or left idle, and the one-way nature of social distribution cannot be broken

---

## Abstract

The previous papers discussed "commodity-boundary control," but one implicit question was never answered head-on: **what exactly is moved out of the commodity category?** This paper's answer: **what is "failed."** On one side, "uncompetitive outdated products" — capacity with zero marginal return that cannot survive market competition; on the other side, "uncompetitive workers" — labor squeezed out of the market that cannot hold a place in competition. Both kinds of elements are judged "failed" by the current system, one destroyed or left idle, the other left idle or relieved. This paper argues: the true meaning of commodity-boundary control is **letting these two kinds of "failed elements" re-pair in a non-market public system** — outdated products absorbed by uncompetitive workers through public labor, producing public resources. This is not a replacement for market distribution, but a **parallel distribution mechanism** activated in the region where market distribution fails.

## 1. The Double Meaning of "Failure"

In capitalism's evaluation system, two kinds of things are judged "failed":

- **Failed assets**: products with zero or negative marginal return — they "cannot be sold" in the market and have lost their commodity value;
- **Failed labor**: workers squeezed out of the market — they "cannot find a place" in competition and have lost their wage value.

**Note: this "failure" judgment is given by the market, a specific evaluation system, not an inherent property of the things.** An "outdated product" fails in the market, but it may still have use-value (recyclable, modifiable); an "uncompetitive worker" fails in the wage market, but still has labor capacity (especially leisure labor).

## 2. The Current System's Handling: Destruction and Idling

The current system handles "failed elements" in only two ways:

| Failed element | Current handling | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Failed assets (outdated products) | destroy / idle / subsidize to limp along | waste / occupy inventory / continuous bleeding |
| Failed labor (uncompetitive workers) | idle / relieve | idle productivity / fiscal burden |

**Both paths lead to the same result: failed elements are wasted.** Outdated products are destroyed, uncompetitive workers are left idle — neither's value is reactivated.

## 3. The True Meaning of Commodity-Boundary Control: Re-Pairing

This paper's core thesis: **the true meaning of commodity-boundary control is re-pairing these two kinds of failed elements.**

- Failed assets (outdated products) → moved out of commodity category → public assets;
- Failed labor (uncompetitive workers) → public labor → exchange public-asset usage rights;
- **The two re-pair in the public system**: outdated products are absorbed by uncompetitive workers through public labor, producing public resources.

**Products are not destroyed, workers are not left idle — the two together form a distribution channel that operates outside the market system but still produces real value.**

## 4. This Forms a "Parallel Distribution Mechanism"

This re-pairing is essentially **activating a parallel distribution mechanism in the region where market distribution fails**:

- **Market distribution**: distribute by price (whoever has money gets);
- **Parallel distribution**: distribute by labor (whoever works gets).

The two do not conflict, but **divide labor**:

- Market distribution manages the **valid region** (positive-marginal-return commodities, distributed by price);
- Parallel distribution manages the **failed region** (zero-marginal-return assets, distributed by labor).

**This is not "redistribution" (robbing the rich to feed the poor), but "creating another distribution system"** — redefining "outdated" and "incapacitated" as a "to-be-connected" state, connecting them to an alternative value-exchange network.

## 5. Falsifiable Corollary

> **Proposition**: if "failed assets" (outdated products) and "failed labor" (uncompetitive workers) can re-pair in the public system, social distribution expands from "market-only one-way distribution" to "market + labor dual-channel distribution," and failed elements are no longer wasted.
> **Contrapositive**: if the two kinds of failed elements cannot re-pair (uncompetitive workers unwilling to join public labor, or outdated products unable to become public assets), they can only be destroyed or left idle, and the one-way nature of distribution cannot be broken.

The converse is falsifiable: if a system successfully re-pairs failed elements yet social distribution remains severely imbalanced, then "failure" is not the only source of imbalance, and there is a deeper structural problem (e.g., the injustice of initial distribution).

## Conclusion

The deep meaning of commodity-boundary control is not "controlling commodities," but **redefining "failure"** — letting the assets and labor the market has sentenced to death come alive again in the public system, re-pair, and produce value again. **It expands "social distribution" from "the market's single channel" to "market + labor dual channels."**
