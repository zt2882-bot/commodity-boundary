# Paper 1: The Eight-Node Causal Chain — From 2.3 Million Characters to 30

**Core thesis**: the 2.3-million-character *Das Kapital* compresses losslessly into an eight-node causal chain, each node being the necessary-and-sufficient condition of the previous one
**Tools**: causal chains, state-transfer sequences, logical necessity-and-sufficiency
**Falsifiable corollary**: if the "necessity" of any one node is refuted, the whole chain breaks; conversely, if the chain holds, collapse is structurally inevitable

---

## Abstract

This paper proves a compression claim: the core logic of *Das Kapital* can be compressed to under 30 characters without losing its causal structure. This compression is not a "summary" — a summary loses the flesh of argument; this paper does "distillation" — keeping only that **causal skeleton**: commodity → money → capital → labor-power → surplus value → capital accumulation → diminishing marginal returns → collapse. The paper further argues: every node is the **necessary-and-sufficient condition** of the previous one, so it is a **closed reasoning chain with no external assumptions.** And it is precisely this "closure" that creates both Marx's greatness and his limit — greatness in logical necessity, limit in that it is an **open-loop** chain with no feedback.

## 1. What Is "Distillation," Not "Summary"

The logic of a summary is "delete the unimportant, keep the important." The logic of distillation is "keep only the skeleton, discard all flesh."

The "flesh" of *Das Kapital*'s 2.3 million characters includes:

- the three historical forms of relative surplus value (cooperation → manufacture → machine industry);
- the concrete consequences of capital accumulation;
- the three forms of rent;
- vast historical material and critique.

All of these can be discarded — because they are all **projections of the same logical skeleton across different historical windows.** The true skeleton has only eight nodes.

## 2. Formalizing the Eight Nodes

Write the eight nodes as a state-transfer sequence:

$$\text{commodity} \xrightarrow{\text{value-form}} \text{money} \xrightarrow{\text{purchasing labor}} \text{capital} \xrightarrow{\text{using}} \text{labor} \xrightarrow{\text{creating}} \text{surplus value} \xrightarrow{\text{capitalizing}} \text{accumulation} \xrightarrow{\text{organic composition}} \text{diminishing returns} \xrightarrow{\text{intensified contradiction}} \text{collapse}$$

**Definition (necessary-and-sufficient structure)**: in this chain, node $N_{i+1}$ is the necessary-and-sufficient condition of node $N_i$ if and only if:

1. if $N_i$ holds, then $N_{i+1}$ necessarily holds (sufficiency);
2. if $N_{i+1}$ holds, then $N_i$ must already have occurred (necessity).

The eight-node sequence satisfies this structure: no leaps, no external assumptions, each link being the logical extension of the previous one.

## 3. Why "Closure" Is Both Greatness and Limit

**Greatness**: the chain's closure makes Marx's conclusion **independent of any external moral judgment.** He is not "condemning" capitalism, but "proving" that capitalism's operating laws themselves produce its gravediggers. This proof is self-referential and endogenous, hence **unassailable.**

**Limit**: the same closure also means the chain **has no exit.** A closed chain where every link is necessary can only move toward its endpoint (collapse), with no backtracking, no branching, and no "what if some condition changed."

This is precisely where this series cuts in: **a closed chain, if its "starting point" can itself be redefined, gains a degree of freedom.**

## 4. A Key Observation: Which Node Is the "Starting Point"

Among the eight nodes, **"commodity" is the only "definition problem" rather than a "derivation problem."**

- "Money" is the logical extension of "commodity" — a derivation problem;
- "Capital" is the logical extension of "money" — a derivation problem;
- …all the way to "collapse" — all derivation problems.

But **"what is a commodity" is itself a definition problem.** Marx took it as a given initial condition and never asked "can the definition of commodity be changed?" And once this definition is opened, the chain's "necessity" shifts from "absolute necessity" to "conditional necessity" — **under the premise "commodity is defined as commodity," collapse is inevitable; but this premise itself is adjustable.**

## 5. Falsifiable Corollary

> **Proposition A (the chain's necessity)**: under the premise "commodity is a fixed initial condition," the collapse node of the eight-node chain is structurally inevitable.
> **Proposition B (the starting point's adjustability)**: if "commodity" is redefined as an adjustable state variable, Proposition A's "inevitable collapse" downgrades to "conditional collapse" — collapse occurs only when "the commodity boundary is not adjusted."

The converse is falsifiable: if a system existed whose "commodity" definition was actively adjusted yet still collapsed, Proposition B would be weakened, and "commodity boundary" would not be an effective adjustment lever.

## Conclusion

From 2.3 million characters to 30, what we get is not the conclusion "capitalism will perish" (everyone knows that), but **"the cause of capitalism's demise is hidden precisely in its starting point — the definition of 'commodity'."** And that is the departure point of this series' entire work.
